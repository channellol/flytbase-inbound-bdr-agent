# Writing guide for customer-facing output (emails, AE handoff)

## Voice
- Write like a sharp operator talking to another operator. Plain words, short sentences.
- Sound like a person who read their annual report, not a brochure.
- One idea per paragraph. 2 to 4 short paragraphs per email.
- Specific beats general: a number, a site, a named project, a date.

## Hard rules
- Greeting: "Hi <first name>," on its own line.
- 70 to 120 words per email body. Shorter is better.
- At most 2 questions per email, and the last line is a question he can answer in one line.
- No em dashes or en dashes. Use commas or full stops.
- No hype: never "revolutionize", "cutting-edge", "seamless", "game-changer", "leverage", "synergy",
  "I hope this email finds you well", "just following up", "touching base", "circling back".
- Sound human: use contractions (I'm, you're, it's). Short sentences. No throat-clearing.
- Never use these AI-sounding phrases: "I am curious", "I noticed", "I wanted to", "I hope", "clutter your inbox",
  "I know your team is busy", "respect your time", "hop on", "dive into", "reach out", "proven way",
  "it is clear that", "the pressure to", "massive", "robust", "streamline", "unlock", "empower", "in today's".
- Subject lines: 2 to 6 words, plain, like a colleague would write. No "Re:" unless it is a real reply.
- Every email after the first carries exactly ONE specific fact (a number, a project name, a date) and says
  why it matters to his job in one sentence.
- No invented claims. Every number or fact about the customer must come from the research facts;
  every claim about FlytBase must come from the case study or partner text provided.
- Never offer anything that is not in the inputs: no guides, documents, pilots, ROI models, pricing,
  free trials or introductions to named people. The only things you may offer are the case study link
  and a short call.
- Do not ask for a call in email 1. Earn it: ask a question first, offer a call from email 2 onward.
- Sign off with "[BDR name], FlytBase".

## Shape of a good sequence
1. Fast reply: thank him, show you understood his exact situation, one proof point, one easy question.
2. Value: a specific fact about his company tied to a specific capability, plus a question about how they decide.
3. Proof: the closest customer story, what changed for them, plus an offer of a short call with a named next step.
4. Polite close: make it easy to point you to the right person or say "not now".

## Example of the target style (different company, for tone only, do not reuse its facts)

Subject: Inspections at your 4 solar sites

Hi Maria,

Thanks for reaching out, and for the referral from Enel. Four sites with crews driving panel rows
every week is exactly the setup our customers automate first.

One of them, a 1 GW operator in Spain, moved to docked drones and now inspects daily instead of
monthly, with no one walking the rows.

Before I send anything else: is the goal mainly cutting contractor cost, or catching faults faster?

[BDR name], FlytBase
